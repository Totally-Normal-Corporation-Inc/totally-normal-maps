What this map tells us

Click a province to see its regions, then a region to see its municipalities. Provinces without regions open directly to municipalities. Use the breadcrumb trail or Back up to return.

Municipalities without a regional grouping appear alongside the regions in their province. Municipal-level areas also include rural and northern statistical equivalents; their official type codes are retained.

Open a city with areas to see its communities, neighbourhoods, arrondissements, sectors or quartiers. A community or arrondissement may open to areas within it. Where a city has separate boundary schemes, use the city-area selector. The coverage list identifies included sources and missing boundaries.

City-area sources can differ from the national city outline. Both are retained for review. Partial coverage is labelled; no area is invented for the remainder. Documented sector names without a qualified boundary source appear in the list without an outline.

Regions use the local division appropriate to each province or territory. County and district groupings describe geography; they do not imply that every community belongs to that government.

The country overview can use a separate cartographic province reference, identified in the attribution. Regional outlines combine member municipal boundaries. Any newer provincial municipality sources are identified separately. Québec's grouping is cross-checked against its administrative regions. Full boundaries are kept separately from the simplified display.

Orange outlines include unapproved source repairs. A region inherits this review requirement from its members.

This catalogue describes geographical coverage. Missing layers and boundaries under review remain explicitly marked.
